Dr. Saiful Khan

B.E., M.S., D.Phil. (Oxford)
Senior Computer Scientist

Scientific Computing, STFC

Dr. Saiful Khan is a Senior Computer Scientist at the Rutherford Appleton Laboratory, STFC, where he leads research and development of data-driven software infrastructure for large-scale scientific applications. He holds a DPhil (PhD) from the University of Oxford, where he conducted postdoctoral research under Prof. Min Chen. His work spans critical national infrastructure including FAIR-MAST (nuclear fusion data systems), RAMPVIS (pandemic response visualization), DAFNI (UK national infrastructure analytics), and SKA (radio astronomy). With industry experience at Oracle, ABB, and other organizations, he bridges advanced research with real-world impact.

Research Interests:
  • Data-driven software infrastructure
  • Large-scale data visualization
  • Large language models (LLM)
  • Agentic AI
Major Projects and Contributions:

VIS4ML4HD: Visualization for Machine Learning for Human-centered Decision Making — The inefficient discarding of non-optimal AI/ML models—and the practice of keeping only a “star model” for each task—arises from our limited ability to understand their skill profiles, combine them effectively, and use them productively. Visualization, by leveraging human visual perception to convey complex, high-volume information more efficiently than statistics or algorithms alone, reduces cognitive load while preserving rich data context, enabling faster and more reliable reasoning. Building on these strengths, this project develops a VIS-enabled infrastructure and toolset that manages large pools of ML models and their profiles, supports the construction of effective model ensembles, and empowers decision makers to interpret and reconcile model anomalies and conflicting predictions. Dr. Khan is a Co-Investigator and researcher on this This EPSRC-funded -funded project, conducted in collaboration with the University of Oxford.

Bridging the Gap: Correlative Imaging Across Length Scales — Understanding protein mechanisms in cancer progression requires integrating data across imaging scales and modalities. This UKRI-funded project combines AI/ML with advanced imaging to analyze patient biobank samples, developing automated workflows to accelerate discovery of mechanisms behind cancer progression and treatment resistance. Dr. Khan is a Co-Investigator of this project in collaboration with the Central Laser Facility (CLF), STFC.

DAFNI: Data & Analytics Facility for National Infrastructure — UK infrastructure research requires modeling and analysis at unprecedented scale. DAFNI is a national computing platform that enables researchers to conduct advanced analysis of models including transport, water, energy, and city-scale systems, providing insights to make UK infrastructure more efficient, reliable, resilient, and affordable.

FAIR-MAST: Nuclear Fusion Data — Nuclear fusion experiments generate massive experimental datasets that remain inaccessible to modern data science methods. Dr. Khan, in collaboration with UK Atomic Energy Authority, led the development of FAIR-MAST for the Mega Ampere Spherical Tokamak (MAST), designing infrastructure that makes fusion data adhere to FAIR principles and open-sources it for visual analytics and AI/ML—the first comprehensive data management system of its kind in fusion research.

RAMPVIS: Rapid Assistance in Modelling the Pandemic — During COVID-19, epidemiologists and modelling scientists needed rapid visual access to pandemic and complex modeling data to make informed decisions. Dr. Khan, in collaboration with University of Oxford and the Scottish Covid Response Consortium (SCRC), designed and developed a visualization infrastructure, RAMPVIS, supporting the scientists of the SCRC, enabling analysis of epidemiological data. He received RAMP Early Career Investigator Award from the Royal Society.

SKA: Square Kilometre Array — The world's largest radio telescope, SKA, generates hundreds of TB/second of raw data, requiring real-time visualization at unprecedented scale. In his postdoctoral work at University of Oxford, Dr. Khan designed and implemented a visual analytics system with low-latency data streaming architecture and web-based rendering pipelines for high-volume real-time data streams.

VBAS: Visual Bulletin Analytics System — Seismological analysis relied on inefficient manual paper-based processes for reviewing complex events across multiple data types. Dr. Khan in collaboration with University of Oxford and International Seismological Centre developed VBAS to integrate various seismological data, e.g., hypocentres, magnitudes, phase arrivals, travel time curves, seismicity maps, station geometry, and more into a unified interactive visualization interface, enabling ISC analysts to detect patterns and anomalies more efficiently and to perform data analysis. The system replaced ISC's 30 year old paper-based system.
